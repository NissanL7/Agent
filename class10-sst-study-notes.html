2 The core idea — why one student gets 95% and another 65%

Everything else in the video is built on this one idea. ≈ 0:00 – 1:45

The half advice we all grew up with

  • “पढ़ते जाओ, रास्ता अपने आप मिल जाएगा” — “Keep studying, the path will find you.” We have heard this line since childhood — in school, in coaching, and even at home.
  • The routine it produces: sit every evening, start from page 1, work hard for two continuous hours, and after closing the book at night, one strange question arrives — “Will whatever I studied in the last two hours even come in the exam?”
  • The mistake is not yours. The mistake is of that half-given advice. It told you that you have to walk — it never told you in which direction to walk.
  • So where does the direction come from? It sounds strange — but the direction is already with you.
The truth: your SST paper is already leaked, and leaked legally. The leaked paper is your NCERT books. Nothing outside it is asked by the paper setter.

Then why 95% vs 65–68%?

  • If the paper is already in everyone's hands — everyone has the NCERT — then why does one child bring 95% while another gets stuck at 65 or 68%?
  • Because both have definitely read the book — but only one has seen it.
  • “What does seeing mean, sir? The same book is open in front of both of us, no?” — Here is what it means:
  • On the same page, one person can see “a question will be made from here”. The other sees nothing at all. He just keeps reading and reading.
  • That one thing is what makes the difference in your marks.

3 The superpower: pattern recognition

The skill that replaces “read everything”. ≈ 1:45 – 2:38

❌ What a normal student does

  • Opens NCERT and sees only text, text, text — “read this paragraph, read this paragraph, read this paragraph”.
  • Feels that the first line of page 1 is as important as the last line of page 50.
  • Keeps reading, memorising and understanding every single line.

Name of this trap: “The Illusion of Equality.”

Result: students burn 90% of their energy in the first two chapters, and by the time they reach the remaining chapters they are completely exhausted.

✅ What a smart student does

  • Does not read NCERT like a normal student — reads it through the examiner's eyes.
  • Understands this clearly: when the CBSE examiner sits down to set your paper, he does not pick questions from just anywhere.
  • It is already fixed in his mind: “I need one 5-mark long answer.”
  • Which means some areas are pre-identified — and those areas can be found.

That is pattern recognition — the superpower.

4 The 4 hacks that tell you where the paper is made from

These are “your permanent glasses for decoding NCERT” — not just tricks. ≈ 2:38 – 4:06

Hack #1

⭐⭐ The 5-Mark Answer Trigger

Rule: anywhere in NCERT, while reading, you find a sub-heading with 4 or 5 points — or a causes / features / impact type paragraph — put a double star (⭐⭐) immediately.

Why it works: the examiner's marking scheme demands 5 points, and these paragraphs fit it perfectly.

Example: “Reasons for the rise of the tertiary sector” — comes as a direct 5-mark or 3-mark question, because the points are plainly visible.

Hack #2

⚖️ The 3-Mark Difference Trigger

Rule: wherever NCERT stands two concepts face to face (opposite each other), a 3-mark question is guaranteed.

Examples he names: Belgium vs Sri Lanka Rabi vs Kharif Formal sector vs Informal sector Credit

Treat every “X vs Y” pair in your book as a 3-marker waiting to happen.

Hack #3

🗺️ The Double-Priority Topic

Rule: if a theory topic in some chapter is also officially in the CBSE map syllabus location-wise, make it a priority.

Examples: Jallianwala Bagh in Nationalism in India; Dams and Minerals in Geography.

Why: it can come in the map and it will come — “History's has to come in the map for sure” — and the same topic will also come in theory. One preparation, two places to score.

Hack #4

🪞 The NCERT Exercise Mirror

Rule: especially in Civics and Economics, 80% of CBSE long answers are lifted directly from NCERT's back exercises — you can talk about any subject.

So: while reading NCERT, if any paragraph looks like a direct answer to a back-exercise question, understand that this topic is going to be copy-pasted into your question paper.

Action: mark every back-exercise question against the paragraph in the chapter that answers it.

6 Book 2 — Civics / Political Science (20 marks)

≈ 6:14 – 8:34

🔑 Why Civics first?

  • In the whole of SST, if there are the easiest and most guaranteed marks anywhere, it is in Civics — because in Civics, direct, direct questions are made.
  • The book comes for the full 20 marks, but marks vary by chapter: 2 to 5 marks per chapter has been seen, going by the previous pattern.
Ch 1 — Power Sharing 2 to 5 marks 5MMCQ3M
  • CBSE's most favourite topic: the Belgium vs Sri Lanka case study — you will have to write their ethnic composition. Simple, simple things.
  • Short, definition-type things that can be asked for 1 mark / 2 marks: What is majoritarianism? What is community government? This kind of thing is asked in short.
  • Lately he has been seeing MCQs being made from “Why power sharing is desirable?”
  • The last topic — “Different forms of power sharing” is very important for theory questions, especially:
    • How power is shared among different organs of government.
    • How power is shared among different levels of government.
Ch 2 — Federalism 2 to 3 marks (sometimes 5) 5M3MMCQ
  • 2 to 3 marks come from Federalism; sometimes 5-mark questions are also asked.
  • Unitary vs Federal system — the same comparison-type question can come in 3 marks.
  • Features of Federalism — directly an important 5-mark part.
  • Coming together vs Holding together federations — asked in comparison type.
  • The topics listed inside Union List, State List and Concurrent List — those topics definitely come as MCQs.
  • Last part: what constitutional amendment happened in 1992 that strengthened decentralisation? — a direct 3-mark / 5-mark question.
  • “So it is sorted — questions come from here and here in this chapter.”
Ch 3 — Gender, Religion and Caste MCQ + theory 5MMCQ
  • Secularism — both MCQ and theory become important from here.
  • How is the oppression of women happening? — a 5-mark question definitely comes from this.
Ch 4 — Political Parties 3M / 5M 5M3M
  • Multi-party system, and after it the comparison — single-party system, two-party system — this becomes an important part.
  • What are the functions of political parties? — again, a direct 5-mark question.
  • National parties and regional parties — these come as “differentiate between”.
  • Challenges to political parties — a potential question; it can be asked challenge by challenge individually, and it also comes directly in 3 marks / 5 marks.
Ch 5 — Outcomes of Democracy usually one 5M question 5M
  • Usually a big theory question does not come from Outcomes of Democracy.
  • But in most cases one question comes: “How is democracy better than other forms of government?”
  • And when it comes, it comes directly in 5 marks.
  • If you just pay attention to the NCERT back-exercise questions here, more than half the work is done.

7 Book 3 — Geography (20 marks)

≈ 8:34 – 11:56

🔑 The one rule for Geography

  • The biggest mistake in Geography: people think it is all just a lot of facts. You are wrong.
  • You have to remember the facts WITH the logic.
  • Example he gives: while teaching the Agriculture chapter in his one-shots he explained that there is a logic behind everything. If cotton grows in the Maharashtra–Gujarat region, it is because black soil is there.
  • So apply the “WHY HERE?” logic to every fact.
  • (And yes — it is true that Geography has a lot of facts.)
Ch 1 — Resources and Development theory + MCQ 3MMCQ
  • The theory part: definition of sustainable development, resource planning, three-step resource planning, the starting part of the chapter, and Agenda 21.
  • MCQ area: in land degradation — in which state, for which cause, land degradation is happening — that comes as an MCQ. Keep these things in mind.
Ch 2 — Forest and Wildlife Resources one 5M + MCQs 5MMCQ
  • Map work does NOT come from this chapter.
  • There are only three buckets in this chapter.
  • Bucket 1 — what steps did the GOVERNMENT take for conservation of forest and wildlife:
    • The Wildlife (Protection) Act becomes important.
    • Project Tiger becomes an important question.
    • MCQs come on things like which tiger reserve is where.
  • Bucket 2 — what steps did the COMMUNITY take for conservation: those five points — around Sariska Tiger Reserve (Alwar district of Rajasthan), the Chipko movement, the Beej Bachao Andolan, and Joint Forest Management — that directly makes one 5-mark question.
Ch 3 — Water Resources 3M / 5M 3M5M
  • A straightforward chapter — also in three parts, like the previous chapter.
  • Part 1 — causes of water scarcity and what its solutions can be. This is the first theory part; the theory question comes from here.
  • Part 2 — dams and rainwater harvesting are also given in this chapter: the question is made on the positives and negatives of dams. This is important.
  • Part 3 — ancient hydrological structures: one question in 3 marks or 5 marks is made from here.
Ch 4 — Agriculture 3M / 5M + objective 5M3MMCQ
  • Types of farming — primitive subsistence farming, intensive subsistence farming and commercial farming — these are asked as “compare between” questions.
  • Different types of cropping season — Rabi, Kharif, Zaid — this also comes in objective: you are given four points and told “identify the type of cropping season”.
  • The crops part: mainly asked only in objective questions. The hack is simple — use the chart he gave and keep things in mind according to that chart.
  • 🔥 But one part IS asked in theory in 3 marks / 5 marks: the Technological and Institutional Reforms topic. DO NOT MISS THIS TOPIC.
Ch 5 — Minerals and Energy Resources 1 theory topic + objective 3MMCQ
  • “Mode of occurrence of minerals” is a direct theory topic — remember this topic for theory.
  • After that it is all minerals and minerals — and all these minerals will be asked only in objective. No big theory question is made from this.
  • The one theory bit in between: why conservation of minerals is important — value-based questions can be made from this chapter in that way.
Ch 6 — Manufacturing Industries theory + objective 3M5MMCQ
  • This is the last chapter (for theory) — because the chapter after it has only map work: Lifelines of National Economy.
  • The moment you open the chapter, the first topic gives four points directly: Importance of Manufacturing — theory comes from here.
  • After that come the individual manufacturing industries. What will be made from them? Not subjective questions — more objective questions.
  • But at the end: what damage are manufacturing industries causing to the environment? — that is a theory segment; theory questions will be made from there.
Ch 7 — Lifelines of National Economy MAP WORK ONLY — no theory Map
  • Theory does not come from this chapter. Only map work comes from Chapter 7.
  • He has already covered its map work in his map-work video.

≈ 11:56 – 14:36

🔑 First correction

“If I say four chapters are coming in this book, even that is wrong” — because the fourth chapter, Globalisation, has only one topic coming from it anyway.

Ch 1 — Development theory + 1 objective 3M5MMCQ

  • What is the World Bank's criteria — it brings out the World Development Report; likewise the Human Development Report brought out by UNDP, whose index treats education, health and income — all three — as important. A theory question comes on this.
  • Apart from this, one objective question comes from the area where charts are given and you have to analyse the data and give the objective answer.

  • This is a good chapter of Economics — the probability of theory questions coming is higher from Chapter 2 and Chapter 3.
  • The three sectors of the economy: comparison between them, or “explain with examples how they are interdependent.” Questions are made on this.
  • Why did the tertiary sector rise in our country? — a question is made on this.
  • Underemployment, GDP — short-answer type questions can be made on these.
  • How to create more employment? — why? Because we see that more people are employed in the primary sector. A question is definitely made from this section.
  • Organised vs unorganised sector — case-based question comes, and differentiate comes.
  • Public sector vs private sector — “compare between” type questions come.
  • One theory question that is important and has been seen sometimes: why is the public sector still important today? — this question is definitely made.
Ch 3 — Money and Credit highest theory probability (with Ch 2) 5M3M1M/2M
  • Money and Credit has many theory parts.
  • Formal sector vs informal sector — these comparison parts become important.
  • Loan activities of banks — an important theory question.
  • Barter exchange? Double coincidence of wants? — these become small definition-type questions.
  • 🔥 Two topics that ALWAYS come in theory:
    • Self Help Groups (SHGs) — what are they? why are they? what is their importance?
    • Why should cheap and affordable credit be given?
Ch 4 — Globalisation only one topic — low possibility 3M
  • Globalisation is in itself a very small chapter.
  • Definition of globalisation, and along with it the two factors that enabled globalisation: technology and liberalisation.
  • Individual questions can come on both, or it can be asked as “factors that have enabled globalisation.”
  • But the possibility is low.

11 The S.P.E. cheat code — the biggest weapon

For the exam hall when your mind goes blank. ≈ 14:36 – 15:14

Situation: suppose that even after knowing everything, in the exam room your mind goes blank. Then the biggest weapon comes in handy — the S.P.E. cheat code.

You are sitting in the exam hall. In front of you is a big 5-mark question — for example “Explain the impact of print culture” or “Impact of globalisation.” Your mind has gone completely blank. You cannot remember at all what to write.

S

SOCIAL

Start writing one point: what difference did it make socially? How did people's thinking change?

P

POLITICAL

Start writing one point: what happened politically? The political impact of whatever is asked.

E

ECONOMIC

Next point: what was the economic impact — on money, on trade, on jobs?

Result: you will see that without knowing anything, you have written a structured answer — written in such a way that you can get 5 marks if you build and write 5 points.

But remember the teacher's own warning: “Friends, there is no substitute for hard work.” When you know the important topics — study them, and practise them. S.P.E. is a rescue line, not the plan.

14 Daily routine & answer-writing rules

⏱️ The 2-hour SST block

  • 0–10 min: open the chapter, look at headings only. Predict questions using the 4 hacks before reading.
  • 10–45 min: read only the ⭐⭐ parts and the “vs” pairs.
  • 45–80 min: write answers from memory — 1 × 5M and 1 × 3M. Closed book.
  • 80–110 min: objective/MCQ round for that chapter; fill the map page if applicable.
  • 110–120 min: match your written answers with the NCERT back exercises (Hack #4).
Never spend the whole block reading, and never start every day from page 1 of chapter 1 — that is the 90%-energy trap.

✍️ Answer-writing rules (from the marking scheme logic)

  • 5-marker = exactly 5 points. That is what the marking scheme demands — so count your points before you stop.
  • Every 5-marker gets 5 numbered points, each in one separate line, each with 1–2 lines of explanation.
  • 3-marker “X vs Y” → always a two-column comparison (basis · X · Y) with 3 differences.
  • 1/2-markers → definition + one example. Nothing more.
  • Case-based → read the source, underline the clue line, answer the question — never write a general essay.
  • Blank? → S·P·E. (Social → Political → Economic.)

📅 Weekly rhythm

  • 6 days study + 1 day revision. On revision day, do only ⭐⭐ points, “vs” pairs and maps — full chapters again are a trap.
  • Two books per day max (e.g. History morning, Economics evening) so the four 20-mark blocks move together.
  • Every Sunday: one map drill + one timed 5-marker test.

15 Exam-hall strategy

  • First 15 minutes: read the whole paper. Mark the questions you can answer at 5/5.
  • Attempt the sure marks first: map work (2 marks are certain), then the “vs” pairs, then the ⭐⭐ 5-markers.
